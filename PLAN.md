# dehip: research and implementation plan

Written 2026-08-01 after a verification session. Every number and claim below was checked against the primary source that day. Nothing here is relayed from a README or a secondhand summary.

## Background and provenance

The trigger was the Rosmine blog post "Fixing LLM writing with Distribution Fine Tuning" (May 2026, archived copy in `~/Downloads/Fixing LLM writing with Distribution Fine Tuning.pdf`). Its claim: SFT-trained models fail to match the training data distribution, and a proprietary post-training step (DFT) fixes this, producing writing that scores 100% human on Pangram and beats an SFT "superbaseline" on every metric.

The DFT algorithm itself is unpublishable-from. The post states it is proprietary and describes only the goal (match the output distribution to the training distribution), never the method. What the post does fully specify is the measurement side, which makes it a usable benchmark spec:

- Base models: Qwen3 instruct 4B / 8B / 14B
- Data: 185K-sample cleaned FineWeb subset, prompts synthesized with Qwen3-32B, outlines with GPT-5-mini, 25% empty outlines
- Eval: 2000 held-out samples (400 for JMQ)
- Metrics: MMD (llama-embed-nemotron-8b embeddings, Gaussian RBF kernel), JMQ (2x pairwise win-rate vs human, GPT-5.4-mini judge, randomized order), token-frequency L2 on 1-grams

The DFT numbers to beat:

| Model | MMD (lower) | JMQ (higher) | Token L2 (lower) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14B SFT superbaseline | 0.037 | 0.49 | 0.0039 |
| 14B DFT | 0.018 | 0.80 | 0.0036 |
| 8B DFT | 0.023 | 0.56 | 0.0031 |
| 4B DFT | 0.025 | 0.40 | 0.0042 |

The superbaseline is a max over a full hyperparameter sweep (132 eval sets per model size), so it is deliberately hard to beat. DFT results are a single fixed configuration.

A second lead, the `harshaneel/humanize` repo's "ceiling" section, pointed at the paper that makes an open alternative possible. A citation audit of that repo's claims (results in REFERENCES.md) found the key citations real and two miscast.

## Core hypothesis

From arXiv 2605.19516 "Base Models Look Human To AI Detectors" (Xu, Zhong, Raghunathan, Fang, Kolter, CMU), verified against the full paper:

- Llama3-8B base output scores 96.7% human on GPTZero and 98.8% on Pangram. The instruct variant scores 30.3% and 17.1%.
- Detectors are tracking artifacts of instruction tuning, not an invariant fingerprint of machine text.

This reframes what DFT is probably doing: not discovering a new distribution, but clawing back the distributional damage that instruction tuning did. If so, the fix does not require retraining the whole model. Restore the base distribution post-hoc with a paraphraser and keep the instruct model for what it is good at (following the prompt, structuring content).

Note the tension with the DFT post, which started from instruct models and rejected base models for poor instruction following. The cascade resolves it: instruct model for content, base-model paraphraser for style.

## HIP recipe (verified from the paper and repo)

HIP trains a base model into a paraphraser that rewrites AI-flavored text toward human prose, then applies it iteratively.

- Training data: ~117K pairs (AI paraphrase -> original human passage) built from RAID and MAGE subsets (abstracts, books, news, wiki). AI side generated by GPT-5-nano. Pipeline: FilterByProvenance -> Normalize -> Deduplicate -> TextQualityScreen, with bounded rejection sampling plus anomaly and semantic-preservation checks.
- Format: plain-text continuation, `<source_text>[AI paraphrase]</source_text><target_text>[human passage]</target_text>`, loss on the target span only.
- Hyperparameters: LoRA rank 128, alpha 128, dropout 0.05. LR 5e-5 cosine. 1 epoch. Effective batch 16. Seq len 2048. QLoRA for 70B.
- Inference: N=10 rounds, temperature 1.0, top-p 0.95, same prompt structure each round.
- Published trajectory: detector human-probability climbs from ~0% to ~100% over 10 rounds. Semantic preservation decays from ~99-100 to ~77-88.
- Cost of full retrain: ~300 GPU-hours on L40S plus ~$200 of API calls.
- Negative result worth remembering: fine-tuning GPT-4.1-nano via the OpenAI API failed to produce a useful trade-off. The base-model starting point appears essential, and API fine-tuning does not expose it.

Code: https://github.com/YixuanEvenXu/humanization-by-iterative-paraphrasing (confirmed live 2026-08-01, includes training, iterative inference, and evaluation utilities matching the paper's GPT-5-nano semantic judge, GPTZero, and Pangram scoring). All 14 LoRA adapters are on HuggingFace under `YixuanEvenXu/` (full list in REFERENCES.md).

## Phase 0: zero-training pipeline

Status: implemented as the `dehip` cascade (`generate` -> `rewrite`), unit- and integration-tested with injected model and hip-run seams. Real-instrument validation on released adapters (the actual smoke run) is pending, tracked in issue #16.

Goal: a working instruct -> HIP cascade with released adapters. No training.

1. Clone the HIP repo (`just clone-hip`).
2. Smoke test with `YixuanEvenXu/Qwen3-0.6B-Base-HIP-adapter`, then move to `Qwen3-4B-Base-HIP-adapter` (the paper found improvements modest beyond 4B).
3. Wire the cascade in `pipeline/`: instruct model (Qwen3-4B or 8B instruct) generates from a prompt, HIP paraphraser rewrites for k rounds.
4. Sanity check on my own drafts with Pangram, which I already use for published prose.

Local constraint: on a 36GB Mac the 0.6B and 4B merged models are fine under llama.cpp or MLX. Anything bigger, or any training, means a rented GPU.

## Phase 1: the Rosmine eval harness

Status: implemented as `dehip score` (with `self-check`, `detect`, and `report`), unit- and integration-tested with injected embedder, judge, and detector seams. All three metrics, the self-check noise gate, the cost gates, and the comparison assembly are built. Numbers on real models and real judge/detector calls are pending (issue #16).

This is the reusable asset regardless of how the cascade performs. Build in `harness/`:

- Token L2: n-gram frequency vectors over model vs reference outputs, Euclidean distance, 1-grams primary.
- MMD: embed with `nvidia/llama-embed-nemotron-8b`, Gaussian RBF kernel, standard unbiased MMD^2 estimator.
- JMQ: pairwise judge, model output vs human reference for the same prompt, randomized A/B order, score = 2x model win-rate. Prompt templates transcribed verbatim from the DFT post's Appendix 9 into `judge-prompts/`. Six dimensions: overall, clarity, coherence, creativity, depth, prompt relevance.

Judge caveat from the DFT post's own Appendix 8: judge models favor LLM output, especially their own. JMQ flatters models. MMD and token L2 do not. Read them together.

## Phase 2: tune iterations for writing quality, not evasion

HIP's N=10 is optimized to beat detectors and pays 15-20 points of semantic drift for it. For prose quality the right k is probably 2-4 with a stopping rule:

- Iterate until MMD or Pangram score plateaus, or
- the semantic judge (shipped in the HIP repo) drops below a floor.

Deliverable: a curve of (k rounds) vs (MMD, JMQ dimensions, Pangram, semantic preservation) that locates the sweet spot. This is the experiment nobody has published: HIP's wins are all detector-evasion numbers, and the open question is whether the rewrites are better prose or just statistically human. JMQ's creativity/depth/clarity dimensions answer exactly that.

## Phase 3 (optional): domain retrain

If the released adapters work but the register is off (they were trained on RAID/MAGE abstracts, books, news, wiki), rebuild the pair dataset from a corpus I care about (my published posts plus a FineWeb blog subset) using the HIP repo's data pipeline, and retrain the LoRA with the recipe above. This is the closest open approximation of "DFT for my use case," at ~300 GPU-hours rented instead of a proprietary service.

## Success criteria

1. Phase 1 harness reproduces sane numbers on known text (human reference vs itself should give MMD ~0, JMQ ~0.5 by construction).
2. The cascade at the Phase 2 sweet spot beats plain instruct output on MMD and token L2, holds semantic preservation above 90, and does not lose on JMQ quality dimensions.
3. Stretch: approach the 14B DFT numbers (MMD 0.018, JMQ 0.80) with a 4B-8B cascade.
